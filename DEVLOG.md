# Development Log

## 2026-09-08

Co-op got its geography. Until now `v` and `j` took a raw coordinate and never looked at where either player was standing, so hosting a run on a distant segment was effectively a teleport and the map had no say in who could play with whom. Both moves now take a direction and behave like a gate-walk that waits: `{"v": {"dir": D}}` opens a visit on the cell through the host's own gate D (and, if issued from inside a run, carries and settles that run first, validated exactly as `gw` is, a survived exit whose replayed gate is D), while `{"j": {"id", "dir"}}` requires the joiner's gate D to open onto precisely that segment. Parties therefore converge on a shared segment from their own sides. Each participant now enters through their own gate and spawns at it, persisted as `visit_participants.entry_direction`, fed into `PlayerSetup.entryDir` and surfaced as `entry_directions` in `getvisitinfo`, so a party arriving from different sides starts apart. On settlement a survivor is left on the far side of the gate they left through with the link recorded, except where the neighbour is unexplored or a foreign provisional claim, in which case they stay in the segment they cleared, since the frontier stays solo. The one-visit-per-segment restriction was dropped as part of this: runs are instances, so two parties, or a party and a soloist, can run the same confirmed segment concurrently, just as two soloists already could. Hosting while already in a visit is still refused. Two supporting fixes came with it. `PlacePlayer` had returned as soon as it found the entry gate without checking whether the mouth tile was already claimed, which never mattered while co-op passed no entry direction at all but would have stacked two participants on the same tile the moment they walked in through the same gate; the gate mouth and room centre are now treated as anchors, first claimant wins, and anyone later falls through to the existing deterministic ring scan. Then the new survivor positioning was found to record a map link into the hub, which has no `segments` row, so it could leave a link row pointing at a cell that is not a segment; settlement now skips that link, matching what a solo gate-walk home already did.

**Decisions & notes:** The spawn change is byte-identical for solo play, since with one participant no tile is ever already taken, and that deliberately includes preserving the existing absence of a wall check on the mouth tile, so already-settled runs still re-verify. Same-gate and mixed-entry spawn vectors are pinned on both the C++ and frontend sides. `SettleThroughGate` was extracted so `gw` and the two visit moves share one settlement path rather than drifting apart.

## 2026-09-05

Co-op Phase 2 robustness landed, then settlement proofs got a lot smaller, then PvP got its first design draft. The robustness problem was a partner who vanishes mid-run: co-op visits on confirmed segments never time out, so the survivor was stranded with no way to settle. The fix follows section 11 of the co-op spec. Settle-confirm (`sc`) now carries `n`, the number of merged-log actions its hash covers, so clients checkpoint periodically; the latest confirm per participant is kept with its length and block height, and a shorter one is refused so confirms only move forward. The settle move (`s`) accepts `solo_from`: the first n actions must hash to every other participant's confirm at exactly that n, that confirm must be at least 20 blocks old (`ABANDON_WINDOW_BLOCKS`), and everything after the split may contain only the submitter's actions. The replay marks the others absent at the split point via a new `DungeonGame::MarkAbsent`: an absent participant's turn passes as if skipped, monsters still act if that participant closed the round, and they are banked as not having exited. The frontend engine mirrors it and a new parity vector (the first 60 actions of the co-op fixture, then a solo continuation) pins both sides. Next, Option A of the action-proofs strategy doc was implemented: the `actions` proof on `xc`, `gw`, and `s` may now be a single compact string instead of a JSON array, with one-letter codes per action type, an optional `*count` repeat suffix, and an actor prefix that is required for merged logs and forbidden for solo proofs. It is expanded before both the replay and the consent hash, so the canonical hash lines in the spec are unchanged. The co-op fixture's compact form is pinned at 593 bytes for 132 actions, roughly a quarter of the JSON. Finally, a Phase 0 draft of the 1v1 duel spec (`docs/SPEC_multiplayer_pvp.md`) was written on top of the shipped co-op machinery: a gold stake in escrow, commit-reveal action choice each round so neither player sees the other's move first, a per-round RNG reseed from both revealed salts, player-vs-player combat composed from the existing formulas in a pinned draw order, last one standing wins without needing a gate, exit as concession, and a refusal to reveal resolved by the abandonment machinery so the staller forfeits the stake. The suite went from 205 to 226 tests.

**Decisions & notes:** The abandonment design deliberately reuses consent hashes as checkpoints rather than adding a new heartbeat mechanism; a live partner keeps checkpointing, which is what keeps their confirm fresh and blocks a premature solo settle. The compact encoding is purely a wire-level change: the hash is computed over the expanded log, so existing confirms and settled runs are untouched, and both forms stay accepted. The PvP spec is a draft with open questions that must be answered before its consensus-critical sections freeze, chiefly whether to keep simultaneous commit-reveal (which doubles round latency) or fall back to alternating initiative, and gold-only stakes for now since item stakes would need the escrow to move inventory rows.

## 2026-09-04

Phase 1 of multiplayer co-op closed out on the backend, in five steps over one evening. First came rewards. Verified settlement had landed the day before, but it still had no answer for how two players split what a run produced. The engine now tracks per-participant damage dealt to monsters (capped at the target's remaining HP so overkill does not inflate a share), accrues every kill's XP to a run-level pool, and, when there is more than one participant, routes monster gold drops into a run-level pool instead of onto the floor. A new `SplitPool` in the settlement layer divides each pool pro-rata by damage share with largest-remainder rounding, ties going to the lower canonical index, and multiplayer claims now verify against those shares. Items and potions stay on the floor for everyone, since they are indivisible and mid-fight pickups are tactics. Next, cross-language parity got its own fixture: `tests/coop_parity_tests.cpp` runs a scripted 2-player game of 132 actions covering every action type, kills by both players, and both exits, and bakes its canonical summary line, pool split, and consent hash on both the C++ side and in the frontend's `npm test`, alongside a settle-hash vector with one entry per action type and the empty-log hash. The RPC's `getvisitinfo` now reports which participants have filed their settle-confirm, so the settling client can see when every `sc` has landed before submitting `s`. The devnet move proxy gained `relay_send`/`relay_recv`, an in-memory per-visit log of each player's own dungeon actions, claim-token checked and capped, which is the first implementation of the frontend's `CoopTransport` interface; the roadmap records that the whole v/j/sc/s lobby and settle flow was verified by a two-browser Playwright run against the devnet. Finally, two known gaps from the phase plan were closed: the host of an open visit can now cancel it with `lv` (every participant is released and the row is kept as cancelled), and `as`, `ui`, `eq`, `uq`, and `di` are refused while a player is in any open or active visit, because the settlement replay runs against on-chain stats and inventory as they stand at settle time, and a mid-visit change would desync the verified run. The suite went from 194 to 205 tests.

**Decisions & notes:** Solo keeps floor gold drops via an explicit N > 1 carve-out, so single-player replay stays byte-identical; the pinned playthrough tests confirm it. The pool split lives in settlement rather than the engine, so it can be rebalanced by coordinated upgrade without forking replay. The relay never orders or writes actions; the engines' round structure and the on-chain consent flow are what make the log binding. The spec now also pins real-time pacing: the round barrier needs no sequencer, waits are self-authored only, the world freezes when everyone idles, and transport is a pluggable dumb pipe (proxy relay for the sandbox, WebRTC or gamechannel broadcast later). Segments default to two players for Phase 1. An active visit still cannot be cancelled by the host; only open ones. The roadmap now lays out the phased plan (spec, 2-player happy path, robustness, true state channels, PvP), with Phase 2 robustness next.

## 2026-09-03

Closed the trust-the-client hole in multiplayer settlement. `ProcessSettle` had been banking whatever xp/gold/kills/loot the results array claimed with no verification; it now requires the merged action log, replays it on the shared N-player engine, and checks every participant's claims against the replay before banking anything, all-or-nothing. Consent is a two-move protocol that rides on the chain's own move authentication, so it needs no wallets: a new `sc` move records a participant's agreement to a merged log by its canonical SHA-256 hash (a plain line encoding, deliberately not JSON, to avoid serialization ambiguity), and any participant may then submit the full `s` move, which executes only once every other participant has a matching confirm on file. The banking step was factored out of `ApplySettlementBody` into a shared per-player `BankPlayerSettlement`, so solo and co-op runs settle through identical on-chain application code. The old trust-based settle tests were replaced by ten CoopSettleTests driving the real protocol through the parser, including forged-log, inflated-claims, missing-confirm, and out-of-turn rejections. 194 tests green.

**Decisions & notes:** Co-op visits are restricted to confirmed segments per the spec. Confirms live only while the visit is active and are cleared on settlement. A log that stops replaying early (invalid or out-of-turn action) rejects the whole move rather than settling a prefix.

## 2026-09-02

Multiplayer co-op went from idea to working engine in a day, spec first. A normative Phase 0 spec (`docs/SPEC_multiplayer_coop.md`) pinned every determinism decision before any code: canonical byte-order participant order, a round structure where each active participant acts once and then monsters take one pass (N=1 degenerating to the exact solo loop), a single shared RNG stream with pinned draw order, nearest-target monster AI with ties to the lower index, the merged action log wire format, and the mutual-consent settlement design. The spec also records why the session RNG is not VRF-based: the stream is consensus machinery that must be derivable from public data, and since the seed is necessarily public before the run starts, a VRF would only change seed derivation without preventing in-run lookahead. The engine refactor followed the same day: `DungeonGame` internals moved from one hardcoded player to a players[] vector, with the entire existing public API kept as participant-0 delegates and every RNG call site keeping its order and gating, so single-player replays stay byte-identical (all 185 existing tests pass unchanged, including the pinned seeded playthroughs). Six new CoopEngineTests lock in spawns, turn enforcement, collisions, and merged-log replay. Separately, the roadmap was updated for the now-live demo at xayarogue.octonion.io, and a deploy annoyance was fixed: Cloudflare was edge-caching the frontend shell and JS for 4 hours, so Caddy now sends `Cache-Control: no-store` for those paths, making deploys visible without a manual purge (images stay cacheable).

**Decisions & notes:** The byte-identical solo gate is the hard compatibility constraint; any N-player change that shifts an RNG draw breaks replay of already-settled runs. Multiplayer channels moved to Next on the roadmap, while MetaMask/wallet integration and calldata optimization moved to a new "Later (production)" section since the demo runs without wallets. VRFs are noted as belonging to the future private-loot layer and PvP commit-reveal entropy, not the session stream.

## 2026-09-01

Closed the last manual step in the sandbox deploy story: the box now redeploys itself when code lands, without a human running `deploy.sh`. The obvious version of this (poll GitHub, redeploy on any new commit) would have been actively harmful, because every redeploy resets the sandbox world and the nightly docs-sweep and dev-log bots commit to these repos most nights. A README tweak wiping the live chain is not an acceptable trade. So `redeploy-if-changed.sh` inspects the incoming commit range and only invokes `deploy.sh` when something that is not markdown and not under `docs/` changed; a docs-only advance is instead fast-forwarded locally, so the next poll doesn't keep re-detecting the same commits. Anything that isn't prose counts as code on purpose, including `deploy.sh` itself, the Dockerfile, the Caddyfile, and `schema.sql`, since all of those change what gets served. A `git diff` failure falls through to deploying, on the grounds that an unnecessary redeploy is cheaper than a silently skipped one, and a repo that has diverged from origin (rather than merely fallen behind) is skipped with a warning rather than force-resolved.

A follow-up fixed the timer, which had been armed with `OnUnitActiveSec`. That directive measures from when the unit last became active, which suits a long-running service but leaves a `Type=oneshot` poll with no next trigger at all after its first run: `list-timers` showed `NEXT: -`. `OnUnitInactiveSec` re-arms five minutes after each poll finishes, which is the correct recurring-oneshot pattern.

**Decisions & notes:** Manual `deploy.sh` still works at any time; the next poll simply finds the repos already current and does nothing. Overlap is handled by systemd, which won't start a second instance of a oneshot while one is running, so a slow deploy can't collide with the next tick. Deploy tooling only, all new files under `devnet/deploy/`; no game logic, so frontend parity and replay verification are untouched.

## 2026-08-29

Made deploying a single command that runs on the box itself. Redeploying previously meant rsyncing a locally built bundle up and hand-running a docker build beside it, which tied every deploy to the state of one particular workstation. `devnet/deploy/deploy.sh` now pulls both repos on the box, builds the GSP image at a pinned commit, smoke-tests that image end to end before anything live points at it, restarts the service, builds the frontend in a throwaway Node container, and publishes it. Two specific hazards got closed along the way. The image clones the GSP inside a RUN layer, and without a build arg that instruction is textually identical between builds, so Docker would happily reuse the cached clone and ship an old GSP while reporting success, leaving yesterday's backend serving today's frontend; `ARG ROG_COMMIT` both pins the build and busts the cache. Separately, the frontend used to be copied over live files, so a page loading mid-deploy could pick up a half-written bundle. Builds now land in `builds/<sha>/` behind a `current` symlink that Caddy's root points at, making the swap one atomic rename.

**Decisions & notes:** Rollback is now a symlink flip, with the last five builds retained. Deploy tooling and docs only; no game logic touched.

## 2026-08-28

The big one: segments lost their surrogate id and are now addressed purely by world coordinate. `(world_x, world_y)` became the primary key of `segments` and the only identity a segment has, in moves, in RPC output, in every foreign key, and in the frontend cache; the hub is `(0, 0)` and keeps no row at all. The id had to go because it was being reused. `logic.cpp` seeded the allocator from `MAX(id) + 1` over the live table, so pruning the highest-id provisional segment handed that same id to the next discovery at a different coordinate, silently repointing every cached reference at the wrong place. Three consequences fall out of the model rather than needing to be bolted on: a gate always leads to the neighbouring cell, so `ProcessGateWalk` resolves its destination by coordinate instead of via a `segment_links` row that could disagree with it; pruning a segment now deletes its visits, so a later segment at the same cell cannot inherit the dead one's history, which in turn moved visit ids onto a monotonic counter persisted in `meta.next_visit_id`; and `d` moves now require a `dir`, since without one there is no cell to claim. Three stale expectations in the adversarial suite were also corrected, as they still assumed a visit timeout fires on a confirmed segment and applies a death penalty, both of which changed back in July.

The day's other fix was a devnet deadlock. The move proxy held `ENV_LOCK` while waiting for a transaction receipt, but anvil runs with `--no-mining`, so a block only exists once something calls `env.generate()`, and both miners need that same lock. The receipt could never arrive while the waiter held it, so every move over 2000 bytes, which is every real settlement, stalled for the full 30 second timeout and queued everything else behind it. Mining the block inside the lock and releasing it before waiting took a 2991-byte move from 30.02s to 0.01s.

**Decisions & notes:** The coordinate refactor changes the move format, so the GSP and the TypeScript frontend must deploy as a pair; the sandbox chain resets regardless, since the schema is incompatible with an existing database. Verified at 185 C++ tests, `devnet/smoke_test.py` end to end, and `devnet/adversarial_test.py` at 50 passed / 0 failed. Deliberately no surrogate id is coming back: an allocated integer gets reused after a prune, which is exactly the bug that started this.

## 2026-08-25

A deployment day, all of it about getting the hosted public sandbox to stand up reliably on a real box. Two problems drove the work. The first was exposure: rather than opening ports and letting Caddy manage certificates, the stack now sits behind a Cloudflare Tunnel, so TLS terminates at Cloudflare's edge and cloudflared forwards plain HTTP to Caddy bound on localhost:80, leaving no inbound port public. The Caddyfile dropped its example.com placeholder for a bare `:80`, and DEPLOY.md was rewritten around the tunnel as the primary path (create/route/config steps, a ufw lock-down section, an off-box reachability check), keeping the direct Caddy-managed-TLS route as a documented alternative. The second problem was the toolchain: the pinned Xaya stack (libxayagame 81249f1, xayax 0e07223) will not compile under the GCC 15 / CMake 4 that newer distros ship. The fix was to containerize the whole devnet (anvil, xayax, GSP, move proxy) on Ubuntu 24.04 / GCC 13 with every component pinned, plus a systemd unit that runs the image and publishes the move proxy on 127.0.0.1:18380 only. SETUP.md was corrected to pin the xayax clone to 0e07223, since upstream restructured its build and removed the root autogen.sh/configure.ac the guide's autotools steps relied on.

**Decisions & notes:** The container makes the build reproducible and independent of the host toolchain; the image was validated by running `devnet/smoke_test.py` end-to-end inside it. Bumping xayax past the pinned commit is deliberately deferred as a separate, test-gated task, not something a deployment does. `ROG_REQUIRE_CLAIM_TOKEN` was removed from the Dockerfile ENV because run_sandbox.sh already defaults it to 1, which also quiets a BuildKit lint that flags any TOKEN-named ENV as a possible secret (it is only a boolean flag). No game logic changed, so frontend parity and replay verification are unaffected.

## 2026-07-30

Reworked what a visit timeout does, in two steps. Previously the 200-block visit timeout force-settled an idle or abandoned run as a full death: half HP, minus 25% gold, plus one death, and a knock-back, which was unfair to a player who had merely disconnected and could cascade (each knock-back opened a fresh run that could time out again). A timeout is now a clean, penalty-free end: no HP loss, no gold loss, no death recorded, with the only cost being that the unsettled run's loot and XP are not banked. The second step fixed the remaining teleport problem: because confirmed segments have no world coordinate to release, their runs are no longer force-settled at all on timeout. The visit stays active and the player resumes exactly where they were via client-side run persistence and reconnect. Only provisional segments still time out, since anti-grief must release their claimed coordinate; that prunes the segment and steps the player back one segment (penalty-free).

**Decisions & notes:** Voluntary forfeit and genuine in-combat death still carry the death penalty; only timeouts were softened. Provisional-segment timeout still prunes the coordinate (anti-grief preserved). Changes are confined to `moveprocessor.cpp` overworld/settlement logic; dungeon generation and combat were untouched, so TypeScript frontend parity is unaffected. Suite stands at 185 tests, with new cases asserting a confirmed active visit does not time out and that a provisional timeout prunes without a death penalty.

## 2026-07-28

Fixed a player-trapping bug in gate-walk. When two segments at adjacent coordinates had been discovered independently, no `segment_links` row existed between them, so walking from one toward the other fell into the discovery path, saw the coordinate already occupied, and was rejected with "coordinate already claimed". The only working exit was back the way the player came. `ProcessGateWalk` now resolves the target by coordinate when no link exists: a confirmed segment there is treated as a free transit (and the missing bidirectional link is created on the spot), another player's provisional segment is rejected, and an empty coordinate still goes through discovery, preserving the coordinate race. `HandleGateWalk` validation mirrors the same logic, and the discovery cooldown moved into the empty-coordinate branch so a free transit can never be blocked by cooldown.

**Decisions & notes:** This completes the free-transit-between-confirmed-segments rule for the unlinked-neighbours case. Overworld logic only; dungeon generation and combat were untouched, so cross-language parity with the TypeScript frontend is unaffected. Two new unit tests cover transit into an unlinked confirmed segment (with link creation) and rejection into another player's provisional; the suite stands at 171 tests.

## 2026-07-27

A design-and-docs day exploring cryptographic privacy features for the game. Two substantial design documents landed. The first specifies player-held-VRF private loot: dungeon loot that stays private to the player until they choose to reveal it, while remaining provably fair. Loot becomes a parallel category kept out of the deterministic replay path and is converted into ordinary on-chain inventory rows only at reveal time. The second maps the Private-Set-Intersection project's tag-mode PSI and commit-reveal dispute layer onto this game's two-layer architecture for fog-of-war: PSI runs only between unfriendly pairs (allies share vision by consent), flights travel as channel messages in two-party dungeon sessions, and the GSP acts as dispute arbiter running a byte-exact audit alongside the existing action-replay verification. The test checklist was also corrected: the multi-agent referee had been reading `gs.players` / `gs.segments` while `getcurrentstate` actually returns them nested under `gamestate`, so earlier "0 referee violations" runs were checking empty arrays and proved nothing.

**Decisions & notes:** The PSI fog-of-war plan phases in four steps with duel PVP first and n-party deferred; open questions include the hostility predicate, N_max, transcript placement, and frontend parity. The vacuous-referee discovery means the multi-agent run needs re-running before its results can be trusted.

## 2026-06-28

A heavy day spanning two themes: tightening the traversal/anti-cheat model to match the agreed design intent, and making the whole stack deployable as a single public sandbox.

On the gameplay side, the big move was overhauling how players cross between segments. Cross-border gate alignment plus entry-gate spawn ("Option B") landed first, so a player always arrives on the matching gate on the other side, with combat-relevant effective stats (str/dex/con/int) now exposed so replay verification stays byte-for-byte in sync with the frontend. Building on that, gate-walk now allows **free transit between already-confirmed segments**: crossing back into known territory (including the hub) is a plain transit with no survive-requirement and no penalty, while stepping into the frontier still requires a genuine completed run. This is the free-transit rule from the traversal design intent, which was also written up in the docs. A first discovery was locked to have no cooldown, and dungeon loot now persists on settle, accompanied by a new discard move for managing inventory.

On the ops side, the stack was made hostable as a single public sandbox origin (Caddy config, a systemd unit, a run script, and a DEPLOY doc), the move rate limit now defaults OFF locally but ON in the hosted deploy, and `roguelike-play` gained a `--solve` mode alongside repairs to stale devnet test scripts. The day closed out with a new test checklist / status doc covering single-player playtests and multiplayer scenarios, with several multiplayer items (contention, access, isolation) marked as now covered by the multi-agent `compete.mjs` harness.

**Decisions & notes:** Free transit between confirmed segments is the agreed target behavior, replacing the prior rule where gate-walk enforced strict settlement for every transit; frontier crossings keep the bail = forfeit + prune anti-grief rule. Any change touching dungeon generation, combat, or stat math must stay in lockstep with the TypeScript frontend, hence exposing effective stats for replay parity. Dungeon loot now persists on settle, but full dungeon persistence (shared vs instanced, monster/item respawn cooldown) remains deferred.
